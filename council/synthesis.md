### Nov. 23, 2026 · Seats: ChatGPT, Claude (cloud), Claude (Corey's browser), Gemini · Orchestrated and verified by Vesper

All four seats received the identical hostile-review brief (five questions: security, Arkansas legality, feasibility, demo gaps, one forced change). Full responses are preserved in this folder (`chatgpt-review.md`, `claude-review.md`, `claude-user-browser-review.md`, `gemini-review.md`) and in the side-chat transcript. Every code-checkable claim below was verified against the actual deploy package before being accepted.

## The verdict (unanimous)

**Proceed with the Citizens Commission and the gated pilot. Do not authorize operational replacement, and do not let anyone describe the current build as production-ready.** The pilot structure, the kill conditions, the honest staff-time accounting, and the ACIC written-answer gate all survived hostile review at every seat. What did not survive is any claim that the accountability architecture is already proven: the council's shared line, in four different phrasings, is that the design demonstrates accountability *at the application layer* and has not yet established it *at the infrastructure layer*.

## Where the council agrees (treat as settled findings)

1. **Janus is a front door, not yet a boundary.** Frigate keeps its own event database, snapshots, clips, UI and API; the host OS and Docker root sit underneath both. Anyone reaching Frigate, the SQLite file, or a backup can read plates with zero Sentinel entries. The honest claim is: *every action mediated by Janus is logged.* Making Janus the only lasting store — Frigate as a write-only sensor, UI/API unreachable by human accounts, media retention matched to 14 days, authenticated ingest on an isolated camera network — is mandatory configuration, not polish.
2. **The unkeyed hash chain has a forgery window.** A privileged insider can rewrite entries since the last published root and recompute the chain. Weekly witnessing leaves up to 7 days exposed, and a witness who cannot recompute (the Clerk holding a hash) or who depends on the same admin's git push is not independent. Direction of travel at three seats: stream entries to a sink outside CPD/admin control as they are written, publish signed checkpoints (with entry counts) daily, and have the auditor recompute independently.
3. **Case validation proves a case exists, not that the search belongs to it.** The realistic misuse is a legitimate case number used to look up a person (the ex-partner scenario came up at three seats unprompted). Needed: a required justification field, automatic flagging of searched plates that never appear in the case file, a ban on catch-all case numbers, audit sampling of searches against case files, and a fail-closed answer for what happens when the open-case list goes stale.
4. **The retention timer collides with evidence law.** Ark. Code § 12-12-1804(b) ties destruction to the conclusion of the investigation or criminal action, which can run months or years; a flat 30-day delete on resolved hits risks destroying evidence, including exculpatory material. The design needs a case/legal-hold flag that stops the retention job, an evidence export to the records/evidence system (hashed, chain-of-custody), and a prosecutor-approved evidence lifecycle. A timer alone cannot own this.
5. **The statutory six-month report is a deliverable the design doesn't yet produce.** § 12-12-1805 requires, every six months and preserved 18 months: plates scanned, names of lists checked, confirmed matches per list, matches that did not correlate to an alert, and matches resulting in arrest *and prosecution*. The dashboard should be built to exactly these categories; "prosecution" requires a feedback loop from the prosecutor's office; and a plate-free outcome record must survive the 14/30-day deletes so the statistics don't get shredded with the reads.
6. **The written policy is legal infrastructure.** § 12-12-1805(a)(3) makes adopted, publicly available rules on obtaining/retaining/destroying data a condition whose breach is grounds to exclude evidence. The policy must be adopted *before the first capture — the lot test included*. Code is not the policy.
7. **Commissioners and the auditor get redacted records, not raw plates.** § 12-12-1808 restricts disclosure of captured plate data (with special protection-order rules). Oversight authority does not itself authorize access to protected data. Auditing is a named, paid or contracted function; the Commission designs policy and receives summaries — it cannot be the unpaid IT department or the weekly log-review body.
8. **Judge the pilot on ground truth, not outcomes.** At CPD's rates, 3 cameras × 90 days projects to roughly 114 matches, ~1 stolen-vehicle recovery, ~3 arrests — noise. Success metrics must be capture rate and OCR accuracy against ground-truthed plates (day/night/rain, plate-text accuracy and jurisdiction accuracy measured separately), misread rate, and uptime. At 8.56 true matches per 100k reads, even a 0.01% false-alert rate adds ~10 false alerts per 100k — the base rate punishes sloppy OCR.
9. **Maintenance ownership breaks before the software does.** Named maintainer + backup, on-call expectations, dead-man alarms (an alert when the retention job, the Frigate bridge, or a witness publication *doesn't* happen — silence must page the Clerk and auditor), camera field work (power, alignment, IR tuning, vandalism), and NDAA §889-compliant cameras with onboard storage and cloud/P2P features off.

## Seat-specific catches worth their own line

- **Claude (Corey's browser):** The Stage 0 lot test may not fit the statute's law-enforcement exception if it matches nothing and isn't run by law enforcement — run it CPD-operated, consenting vehicles, under the adopted policy. The protection-order flag must exist *before* any registered-owner disclosure feature (an abuser who co-owns the victim's car is the scenario). And: **CPD's own Flock audit log can show how many of the 7 recoveries and 19 arrests depended on data older than 14 days or on other agencies' cameras** — the strongest available evidence for or against the 14-day line, and it is requestable now.
- **Gemini:** SQLite `DELETE` doesn't erase — rows persist in free pages and the WAL unless `secure_delete` is on and the file is vacuumed. **Verified and fixed the same day (below).** Also § 12-12-1807's private right of action ($1,000 liquidated damages per injured person) prices the downside of getting § 12-12-1803 wrong, and a political catch: *"If the stack is already built, what is the Commission designing?"* — the build must be presented as a reference implementation the Commission can amend or reject, not a finished answer.
- **ChatGPT:** A failed pilot must not default back to renewing the vendor. Whether Cabot has ALPR at all and who operates it are separate questions, and the authorization should say so.

## Fixed already (verified live, Nov. 23, 2026)

1. **No plate text in the Sentinel chain.** `HOTLIST_ADD` / `HOTLIST_REMOVE` entries now carry a keyed HMAC plate reference (`plate_ref`, key from `NOCTUA_AUDIT_KEY` or a per-install key file) instead of the plate. Re-tested end to end: hot-list add → alert → remove → chain verify, with a raw scan of the audit table showing zero plate text and matching references across add/remove.
2. **Real deletion at the file layer.** `PRAGMA secure_delete = ON` in Janus, Sentinel, and the retention job, plus a `VACUUM` after any retention run that deletes rows. Re-tested with a forced 0-day retention run: a deleted read's plate occurs **zero times** in the database file afterward; the chain still verifies; the only remaining copies of the test plate were the live hot-list entry itself, which retention correctly does not touch. Backup retention must follow the same schedule — now stated in the retention job and flagged for the runbook.

## Disagreements (preserved, not averaged)

- **The one forced change differs by seat.** ChatGPT: independent adversarial verification of the whole data-access boundary as a binding pre-deployment condition, unresolved critical bypass = no deployment. Claude (cloud): stream Sentinel to an independent sink in real time + signed daily checkpoints. Claude (browser): close the Frigate bypass and add a *bypass test to the demo itself*. Gemini: move the ACIC determination and the City Attorney's § 12-12-1803 opinion to **before the item reaches a council agenda**. The first three are compatible and can all be adopted. **Gemini's is a sequencing change that conflicts with the current plan** (Commission chartered first, ACIC answer as a deployment gate): Gemini argues a Path C answer or a Path B price above the Flock lease kills the build after the political capital is spent. This is Corey's call, not the council's.
- **Tier 3 judicial authorization.** All seats agree it exceeds Arkansas law and that Arkansas has no ALPR court-order mechanism (in practice it means a probable-cause warrant). Claude seats treat it as a legitimate policy choice to define precisely; ChatGPT and Gemini weight the failure mode more heavily — if judges won't sign or CPD can't work inside it, every urgent query funnels through the supervisor emergency flag and the exception becomes the rule. Minimum agreed mitigation: post-hoc judicial/auditor review of every emergency flag and emergency-flag frequency tracked as a kill metric until the two-key workflow exists.
- **Path A realism.** Claude (browser) and Gemini both doubt dispatch/CAD can ingest outside reads and match in real time, which would collapse the choice to Path B (priced) or Path C. The spec treats Path A as the planning assumption. Resolution is factual, not rhetorical: it becomes an explicit question in the ACIC/dispatch inquiry (added to the amendment list).

## Spec v2 amendment list (drafting instructions)

1. Adopted written policy (§ 12-12-1805(a)(3)) is a precondition to **any** capture, Stage 0 included; Stage 0 is CPD-operated with consenting vehicles only.
2. Frigate lockdown as a design requirement (write-only sensor; no human-reachable UI/API; media retention ≤ 14 days; authenticated ingest; isolated camera VLAN; NTP discipline).
3. Sentinel v2: real-time streaming to an independent sink; daily signed checkpoints including entry count; auditor recomputes independently; chain stores keyed plate references only (done in code).
4. Evidence lifecycle: legal-hold flag in Janus (build), evidence export with hash + chain of custody, prosecutor-approved disposition schedule replacing the flat 30-day resolved-hit delete; plate-free outcome records preserved 18 months for the statutory report.
5. Statutory report generator: the public dashboard's report view = § 12-12-1805 categories exactly, compiled six-monthly, preserved 18 months, aggregates exempt from retention deletes; prosecutor feedback loop for prosecution outcomes.
6. Case-gate hardening: justification field required; catch-all case numbers banned and auto-flagged; searched-plate-vs-case-file sampling by the auditor; case-list staleness fails closed with the failure itself logged and alarmed; emergency-flag post-hoc review + frequency kill metric.
7. Protection-order suppression flag built **before** any owner-disclosure feature; owner-disclosure request process defined (registered owner is a permitted recipient); FOIA redaction process for the audit log that preserves public verifiability via published roots.
8. ACIC/dispatch inquiry expanded: Path A real-time matching capability and latency, CJIS scope via case-list sync and via hit-detail return flow, hot-list 24-hour update duty (§ 12-12-1804(c)) ownership.
9. Named maintainer + deputy, on-call expectation, dead-man alarms to Clerk and auditor for retention/bridge/witness failures; Commission charter language: designs policy, receives summaries; auditing is a named paid/contracted role.
10. NDAA §889 camera requirement; onboard storage and cloud/P2P disabled.
11. Independent adversarial verification of the data-access boundary as a written condition in the authorization (ChatGPT's forced change), scope: admin access, Frigate, backups, ingest bridge, witness circumvention.
12. Demo script additions: a bypass attempt shown blocked/logged; on-screen disclosure that the console is not yet wired to the production API; the tamper test re-run as a full-chain rewrite against the witnessed checkpoints.
13. Authorization language: a failed pilot does not default to vendor renewal; discontinuation remains an explicit option.
